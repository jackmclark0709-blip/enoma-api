The short answer

It depends far more on who builds it than on what's in it. A small service business can spend almost nothing a month or several thousand dollars up front for sites that bring in a similar number of calls. Here's how the main options compare.

What a contractor website actually needs

For a plumber, landscaper, electrician or painter, a website has one job: turn someone searching for your service into a phone call or a quote request. That takes:

  • Your services, each named the way customers search for them.
  • The towns you serve.
  • Your phone number, one tap to call on mobile.
  • A short quote form that comes straight to you.
  • Trust signals: reviews, licensed and insured, photos of real jobs.
  • Fast loading and clean setup so Google can read it.

That's it. Blogs, ten pages of "About us," animations and stock photos don't get you more calls. They mostly add cost.

Hidden costs to ask about

  • Who makes changes? If every phone number or service update means emailing a developer and waiting, that's a real cost.
  • Hosting and domain renewals that start cheap and renew higher.
  • Who owns the site if you stop paying an agency.
  • Lead sharing. Some "free website" offers come from lead platforms that also send your visitors to competitors.

Common questions

Contractor website costs: common questions

How much should a small contractor pay for a website?

It ranges from a small monthly fee for a DIY builder or lead-page tool to several thousand dollars for an agency build. For most small service businesses, a simple page with your services, towns, phone number and a quote form is enough to bring in calls, so there's no need to start with the expensive option.

Do I need to pay monthly for a website?

Almost every website has some ongoing cost for hosting and a domain, even if the build was a one-time fee. The question is whether the monthly price also covers updates and support, or just keeps the site online.

Is a free website worth it?

Check who's paying for it. Some free website offers come from lead platforms that also show your visitors to competitors or own the site if you leave. Make sure leads go only to you.
