1. Get found when people search

Homeowners search "lawn care near me," "lawn mowing service" or "fall cleanup" plus their town. Two things decide whether they find you: your Google Business Profile (the map results) and a website that clearly says what you do and where. If you only have a Facebook page, you're mostly missing these searches.

  • Set up your Business Profile with the right category ("Landscaper" or "Lawn care service"), your service area and real photos.
  • Have a simple website listing each service and every town you cover, linked from that profile.

2. Make reviews part of the routine

Ask for a Google review after the first cut of the season, after a big cleanup, or when a customer compliments the work. Send a text with the direct link. A steady trickle of fresh reviews does more than a burst once a year.

3. Build density street by street

The most profitable lawn care routes are tight. When you land a customer, knock on or leave a door hanger at the neighbors' houses: "We already service your street on Tuesdays." Offering a small discount for neighbors is a common way to fill a block.

4. Sell the seasons, not just the mow

Your existing customers are your cheapest new revenue. Offer spring cleanups, aeration and overseeding, mulch, fall cleanups and snow removal before they think to look elsewhere. Put each of these services on your website so new customers searching for them find you too.

5. Answer quote requests fast

Most landscaping work starts with "how much for my yard?" The company that replies first often gets the job. A quote form that sends leads straight to your phone helps you answer the same day.

6. Partner locally

Realtors, property managers, pool companies and fence installers all meet homeowners who need yard work. A simple referral arrangement can send steady work your way.

Common questions

Getting lawn care customers: common questions

How do I get lawn care customers fast?

Finish your Google Business Profile, ask current customers for reviews, put up a simple website listing your services and towns, and canvass the neighbors of every customer you already have. Those steps cost little and can bring calls within weeks.

Do door hangers and flyers still work for lawn care?

Yes, especially on streets where you already have customers, because you can offer the convenience of being there every week anyway. Pair them with a website so people who keep the flyer can look you up.

Is Facebook enough to get lawn care customers?

Facebook is good for showing your work to people who already know you, but it rarely shows up when a stranger searches Google for lawn care in their town. A website plus a Google Business Profile covers those searches.
